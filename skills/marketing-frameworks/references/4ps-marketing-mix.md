# 4 Ps of Marketing (the marketing mix)

**What it is:** The four levers a business controls when taking a product to market: **Product, Price, Place, Promotion**. Neil Borden popularised the "marketing mix" idea in the 1950s-60s; E. Jerome McCarthy condensed it to the 4 Ps in 1960.

## The four Ps

| P | Key questions |
|---|---|
| **Product** | What problem does it solve? Features, quality, design, branding, packaging, range, warranty, service |
| **Price** | Cost-based, competitor-based or value-based? List price, discounts, bundles, payment terms, perceived value |
| **Place** | Where and how do customers buy? Channels, retail vs online vs marketplace, coverage, logistics, inventory |
| **Promotion** | How do they hear about it? Advertising, PR, social, content, sales, email, offers, influencers |

## Extended versions

- **7 Ps** (Booms & Bitner, 1981) for services: add **People, Process, Physical evidence**.
- **4 Cs** (Lauterborn, 1990), the customer-side view: **Customer value, Cost, Convenience, Communication**. Useful as a check that each P makes sense to the buyer.

## How to apply it

1. Start from STP. The mix must fit the target segment and the position. Every P should support it.
2. Fill a four-row table for the current state, then the proposed changes.
3. Check consistency: a premium position with discount-bin Place and coupon-heavy Promotion is incoherent.
4. Use the 4 Cs as a sanity check from the customer's side.

## Worked example

A premium skincare line positioned as clinical and effective: Product = dermatologist-tested, minimal clean packaging. Price = above mass market, no deep discounts. Place = own site plus selected pharmacies, not supermarkets. Promotion = expert-led content, before/after case studies, dermatologist partnerships.

## Common mistakes

- Treating the Ps as separate departments rather than one consistent system.
- Over-weighting Promotion (it cannot fix a weak Product or wrong Price).
- Ignoring service elements (use the 7 Ps).

## Evidence

Foundational teaching model. Critiqued as seller-centric and product-era, which is why the 4 Cs and 7 Ps exist. Still the fastest checklist for a go-to-market plan.
