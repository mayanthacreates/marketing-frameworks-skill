# Playbook: social media

**When to use this file:** social media strategy, content pillars, calendars, hooks and captions, paid social, community management, short-form video, or an audit of an existing account. Platform-neutral: adapt the tactics to Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn, YouTube, X, Facebook or others.

Why this order: social is where Reach happens and where most attention is wasted. Knowing who you are talking to (STP) and what behavior you want (TPB) keeps posts from becoming noise. The brand stage decides the content mix; the touchpoint idea and the Eisenhower matrix keep the calendar realistic. Apply a step only if the account or goal calls for it.

## Workflow

1. **Set the goal and the behavior.** Not "grow followers" alone: which action should social cause (visit, enquire, book, subscribe, share)? Take the baseline from the account's own analytics; with none, set up tracking and use a 2-week baseline ([RACE](race-framework.md)).
2. **STP for the account.** Pick the audience segment and write the positioning statement. The bio is that statement shortened ([STP](stp-model.md)).
3. **Pick the brand pyramid stage** ([brand pyramid](brand-resonance-pyramid.md)):
   - Unknown account: Identity (salience). Be recognisable, repeat distinctive assets.
   - Known but not clearly defined: Meaning. Show performance (what it does, demos) and imagery (personality, who it is for).
   - Known but not trusted: Response. Build judgments (proof, reviews, credibility) and feelings (warmth, belonging).
   - Trusted: Relationships (resonance). Community, repeat engagement, member or customer stories.
4. **Choose 3-5 content pillars** from that stage and the audience's needs. Map each to a RACE stage: Reach (shareable, discovery), Act (useful, saveable), Convert (offer, proof), Engage (community, customers).
5. **TPB diagnosis for the behavior.** Which factor blocks it: attitude, norms or control? Use 5-10 short customer chats, a story poll or DMs and comments; if no data, state the assumption. TPB is a diagnostic checklist; moving a factor may not change behavior, so test ([TPB](theory-of-planned-behavior.md)).
6. **Map the touchpoints.** Social is one touch in a longer path. Plan a sequence across posts, stories, email and the website, with frequency caps on paid and varied creative. No magic number of views ([rule of 7](rule-of-7.md)).
7. **Write hooks and captions.** Hook = curiosity gap or clear benefit. Open loops ("part 2 on Thursday") need a payoff, or trust drops. The caption addresses the weak TPB factor and the call to action makes the next step easy ([Zeigarnik](zeigarnik-effect.md)).
8. **Build the calendar** with the template below, balancing pillars and stages across weeks.
9. **Prioritise with Eisenhower.** Evergreen and series content is Schedule; reactive trend posts are Do or Drop ([Eisenhower](eisenhower-matrix.md)).
10. **Paid social, only if used.** For anything beyond a boosted post, switch to [apply-paid-ads.md](apply-paid-ads.md) (CAC, ROAS, attribution, fatigue; metrics in [paid-media-metrics.md](paid-media-metrics.md)). Segment audiences by need, cap frequency, rotate creative, retarget warm audiences. If showing tiers, test any decoy (worse on every attribute than the target, honest options) as an A/B hypothesis ([decoy](decoy-effect.md)).
11. **Community and creators.** Set a reply window, for example same business day, and feature customers (norms). For creators and staff, brief with specific, high but believable expectations plus support. Small, contested effect; a briefing standard, not a growth lever ([Pygmalion](pygmalion-effect.md)).
12. **Review monthly.** Compare to baseline, double down on the pillar and format that moved the behavior, cut the rest. Pause a format if cost or effort per result is worse than baseline for two review cycles.

Fits weakly: **decoy** only matters if posts or ads show pricing options. Use the **4 Ps** once for the account (what Product, Price, Place and Promotion social supports), not per post ([4 Ps](4ps-marketing-mix.md)).

## Framework to tactics to measures

| Framework | Tactics on social | Measure |
|---|---|---|
| STP | One audience, positioning-led bio, content speaks to their need | Follower and engager profile vs target segment |
| TPB | Hook for attitude, testimonials and UGC for norms, link-in-bio and simple steps for control | Link clicks, enquiries, bookings, DM-to-order rate |
| RACE | Pillars mapped to stages | Reach and impressions; saves, shares, profile visits; orders; repeat buyers and replies |
| Brand pyramid | Pillar mix chosen by the weakest stage; distinctive assets repeated | Branded search, mentions, review count, community activity |
| Rule of 7 | Sequenced posts across formats, retargeting with caps, creative rotation | Frequency, CTR trend, cost per result trend |
| Zeigarnik | Series and open loops, "finish your booking" prompts | Series return rate, watch-through, retarget conversions |
| Eisenhower | Calendar triage, batching Schedule items | Posts shipped on plan, effort on top pillars |
| Decoy | Tiered offer creative vs plain version | Target-option share |
| Pygmalion | Creator and staff briefs, feedback loops | Output quality, revisions needed |

## Worked example: yoga studio, 4-week plan

Business: "Still Point", a neighbourhood yoga studio with 3 teachers. Goal: more first-class bookings from locals new to yoga. Baseline: weekly first-class bookings from the booking tool and profile visits from account analytics, recorded for 2 weeks first.

- **Audience and position:** "For beginners, Still Point is the neighbourhood studio where your first class is paced for you, with small groups and a teacher who walks you through each pose."
- **Brand stage:** Meaning. People half-know the studio but not what a beginner class is like, so show performance (real beginner classes) and imagery (friendly, unintimidating). Reviews come next (Response).
- **TPB assumption (no survey yet):** attitude fine; norms ("everyone else is experienced") and control ("what do I wear or do?") are weak.
- **Pillars:** (1) Beginner basics (Act, Meaning: performance), (2) Student stories (Convert, norms), (3) Studio life and teachers (Engage, Meaning: imagery), (4) Quick home sequences (Reach).

Week 1, in the template format:

| Week | Day | Platform | Hook | Format | Owner | Metric |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mon | Instagram | "The 3 questions every beginner is afraid to ask" | Carousel | Teacher A | Saves, first-class link clicks |
| 1 | Wed | TikTok and Reels | "One pose, three ways: pick the one that suits you" | Short video | Front-desk helper | Watch-through, profile visits |
| 1 | Thu | Instagram | "Poll: what worries you about a first class?" | Story poll | Teacher B | Poll replies (feeds TPB diagnosis) |
| 1 | Sat | Instagram | "Come as you are: what to wear and bring" | Reel | Teacher A | Bookings in the following 48 hours |

Weeks 2-4 in one line each:
- **Week 2:** consented student story post, "Part 1 of 3: poses I wish I knew at the start" with parts 2 and 3 scheduled (open loop with a dated payoff), Friday reminder story.
- **Week 3:** studio tour reel, teacher intro, buddy-pass offer to bring a friend (norms and control).
- **Week 4:** series recap, pinned FAQ highlight, review ask to attendees (Response), retarget video viewers with the booking link at a capped frequency.
- **Eisenhower:** Do: pinned FAQ and booking link. Schedule: series and reviews. Drop: chasing every trend audio.
- **Review:** bookings and profile visits vs baseline, saves and shares per pillar, which hook style drove booking clicks.

## Common mistakes

- Chasing followers instead of the behavior that earns revenue.
- Posting in every format and platform with no audience or pillars.
- Open loops that never pay off.
- Quoting a posting frequency or engagement rate as a universal benchmark instead of your own history.
- Treating the rule of 7 as a posting quota.
- Heavy community talk before anyone can recall or trust the brand (Relationships before Identity and Response).
- Copying a competitor's content without checking it fits your position.
- Ignoring replies and comments, where norms and trust are built.
- Putting everything in "urgent" trend reactions and dropping the compounding series.
