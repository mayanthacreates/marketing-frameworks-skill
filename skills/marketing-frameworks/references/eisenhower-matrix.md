# Eisenhower Matrix (urgent vs important)

**What it is:** A 2x2 grid for prioritising tasks by urgency and importance. Named after Dwight D. Eisenhower, from a 1954 speech at Northwestern University where he quoted a former college president: "I have two kinds of problems, the urgent and the important. The urgent are not important, and the important are never urgent." Stephen Covey turned it into the four-quadrant tool in *The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People* (1989).

## The quadrants

| | Urgent | Not urgent |
|---|---|---|
| **Important** | **Do** - crises, deadlines, client fires | **Schedule** - strategy, brand building, SEO, learning |
| **Not important** | **Delegate** - most emails, routine requests, some meetings | **Delete** - busywork, vanity reporting, endless scrolling |

## How to apply it

1. Brain-dump every task.
2. Important = moves a real goal forward. Urgent = has a near deadline or a consequence if delayed.
3. Place each task. Be strict: most things are less important than they feel.
4. Block calendar time for the Schedule quadrant first. That is where growth lives, and it never shouts.
5. Review weekly.

## Marketing uses

- Content backlog triage: evergreen SEO pieces are usually Schedule, trend reactions are Do or Delete.
- Campaign roadmaps: separate firefighting from compounding work.
- Client requests: decide what to push back on or delegate.

## Common mistakes

- Letting Do swallow everything because everything is "urgent".
- Never actually deleting anything.
- Confusing urgent-for-someone-else with important-for-you.

## Evidence

A heuristic with no formal research base. Its value is forcing an explicit trade-off.
