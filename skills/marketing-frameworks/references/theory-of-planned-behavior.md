# Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB)

**What it is:** Icek Ajzen's model (1991, *Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes*), extending his earlier Theory of Reasoned Action with Martin Fishbein. Behavior is predicted by **intention**, and intention is driven by three factors:

| Factor | Meaning | Marketing question |
|---|---|---|
| **Attitude** | Do I think the behavior is good or bad for me? | Do they believe the benefits outweigh the costs? |
| **Subjective norms** | Do people who matter to me approve? Are others doing it? | Does it feel normal and approved by their peers? |
| **Perceived behavioral control** | Can I actually do it? | Is it easy, affordable, possible for them? |

Perceived behavioral control also affects behavior directly: people who intend to act still fail if they lack the ability or opportunity.

## How to apply it

1. Define the exact behavior (specific action, target, time). "Book a test drive this month", not "like our brand".
2. Find which factor is the bottleneck. Interview or survey: "Would it be good?" (attitude), "Would people you know approve?" (norms), "How easy would it be?" (control).
3. Use matching tactics:
   - Attitude: benefits, demonstrations, reframing costs, guarantees.
   - Norms: testimonials, "X people in your area", expert or peer endorsement, UGC.
   - Control: remove friction, instalments, free trials, step-by-step guides, booking in two taps.
4. Close the intention-behavior gap with reminders, deadlines and commitment devices.

## Worked example

Getting gym members to try the new 6am class: attitude is fine ("good for me"), norms are fine, but control is low ("I can't wake up / don't know the moves"). The fix is a beginner-labelled first class, a reminder the night before and a buddy pass, not more motivational ads.

## Common mistakes

- Pouring effort into attitude ("convince them") when the real barrier is control (friction, price, access).
- Measuring intention and assuming behavior will follow.

## Evidence

Strong. One of the most tested models in social psychology. Armitage & Conner's 2001 meta-analysis (185 studies) found TPB explained about 39% of the variance in intention and 27% in behavior. It predicts intention much better than actual behavior, which is why the intention-behavior gap tactics in step 4 matter.
