# Brand Resonance Pyramid (Keller's CBBE model)

**What it is:** Kevin Lane Keller's Customer-Based Brand Equity model (introduced in Keller, 1993, *Journal of Marketing*, and set out as the pyramid in his 2001 Marketing Science Institute paper "Building Customer-Based Brand Equity", MSI Working Paper 01-107). Strong brands are built by moving customers up four stages, from "who are you?" to "I'm loyal and I'd defend you".

## The pyramid (bottom to top)

| Stage | Customer question | Building blocks | Goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Identity | Who are you? | **Salience** - awareness, recall in buying situations | Deep, broad awareness |
| 2. Meaning | What are you? | **Performance** (functional: reliability, features, price, service) and **Imagery** (user profile, personality, values, history) | Points of difference and parity |
| 3. Response | What about you? (What do I think and feel?) | **Judgments** (quality, credibility, consideration, superiority) and **Feelings** (warmth, fun, excitement, security, social approval, self-respect) | Positive, accessible reactions |
| 4. Relationships | What about you and me? | **Resonance** - behavioral loyalty, attitudinal attachment, sense of community, active engagement | Intense, active loyalty |

The left side (performance, judgments) is the rational route. The right side (imagery, feelings) is the emotional route. The strongest brands build both.

## How to apply it

1. Audit the brand block by block with real data: awareness and recall surveys, reviews, social listening, repeat purchase.
2. Find the lowest weak block. Resonance cannot be built on a weak base. A brand nobody recalls has a salience problem, not a loyalty problem.
3. Pick actions per block: salience (reach, distinctive assets), performance (product fixes), imagery (story, ambassadors), judgments (proof, reviews), feelings (tone, experiences), resonance (community, loyalty programs, user-generated content).
4. Re-measure over quarters, not weeks.

## Worked example

A new sneaker brand: Salience is low, so the first job is distinctive design assets and reach. Meaning comes from comfort claims backed by tests (performance) and a running-club culture (imagery). Response is earned through reviews and the feeling of belonging. Resonance comes from club events, member drops and owners posting their runs.

## Common mistakes

- Jumping to community and loyalty programs before people know or rate the brand.
- Only emotional branding with no performance proof, or the reverse.

## Evidence

A widely taught and used academic model. Diagnostic (a way to structure brand audits), not a predictive theory.
