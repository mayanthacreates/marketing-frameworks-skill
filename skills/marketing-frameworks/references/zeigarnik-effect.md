# Zeigarnik Effect

**What it is:** The claim that people remember unfinished or interrupted tasks better than finished ones, because an open task keeps a mental "tension" active. Named after Bluma Zeigarnik, who studied it in Kurt Lewin's lab in Berlin (published 1927). Closely related: the **Ovsiankina effect** (Maria Ovsiankina, 1928), the urge to go back and finish an interrupted task.

## Where marketers use it

- **Progress bars and profile completion** ("Your profile is 70% complete").
- **Onboarding checklists** with some steps already ticked.
- **Abandoned cart and "finish your booking" reminders.**
- **Cliffhangers and open loops** in video, email series, carousels ("Part 2 tomorrow").
- **Curiosity-gap hooks** in short-form content.

## How to apply it

1. Show the user a task as *started* and *incomplete*, with a clear, small next step.
2. Make finishing easy (one click back to where they left off).
3. Close the loop you opened. Teasers that never pay off train people to ignore you.
4. Keep reminders few and useful. Nagging becomes spam.

## Evidence

Weak for the memory claim, stronger for resumption. A 2025 meta-analysis by Ghibellini & Meier (*Humanities and Social Sciences Communications*) found no general memory advantage for unfinished tasks, while the tendency to resume interrupted tasks (Ovsiankina) held up. So the solid basis for progress bars and "finish what you started" reminders is the **urge to complete**, not better memory. Many sources still quote the memory version as fact. Correct it gently.
